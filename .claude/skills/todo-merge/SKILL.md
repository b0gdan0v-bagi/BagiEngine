---
name: todo-merge
description: Merges a reviewed todo/<id> branch (left by todo-loop in status review) into the base branch of its repository. Prints a short report of what was done, asks the owner whether to merge, and only after a yes merges with --no-ff, closes the entry in the todo store (as `/todo-loop close` does), files the review follow-ups, deletes the branch, writes what the repository's rules ask with a merge (a changelog fragment and a version, when it has them) and, for an area that is a submodule of another (for example engine/ in a game), commits the moved pointer. A branch that conflicts goes back to its worker as a conflict round after the owner's yes. Use when the user asks to merge a todo branch, accept the result of the todo loop, says "смержи ветку", "todo-merge", or wants a report before merging a todo branch.
---

# todo-merge

You are the **conductor** and do everything yourself, no agents. The skill takes one branch `todo/<branch-id>` (or `feature/<umbrella id>`) whose entry `todo-loop` left in status `review` in the todo store, shows the owner what it holds, **asks**, and only then touches the base branch. The store and its CLI are the store's docs that section `Areas` of the rules file names; the closing steps are in `.claude/skills/todo-loop/SKILL.md` ("Closing merged entries").

`/todo-merge [<id or branch>]`

The terms `<area>`, `<R>` (repository of the area), `<base>`, `<engine>` and `todo` (`python3 <engine>/tools/todo/todo.py --actor todo-merge`) are the ones of `todo-loop/SKILL.md` ("Areas and the repository rules"), taken from `.claude/rules/todo-project.md`. Read that whole file first; below `worker.py` is `python3 <engine>/tools/todo/worker.py`. Its sections used here: `Areas` (the areas, the root), `Build and test (baseline and reviewer)` (the tests after a moved base), `Shots` (the pictures of step 4), `Changelog and version` (what a merge writes besides the merge commit), `Shared submodule` (the pointer commit), `Workers` (the shared docs). A section that is missing counts as `none`; without `Areas` stop.

**Changelog and version.** Section `Changelog and version` of the rules file says, per area, what a merge writes besides the merge commit (for example a changelog fragment and a version number from the store's counter) and how: the draft and the forecast of step 4, the question of step 5, the merge step of step 6 when the base moved, the commit of step 8, the name of the pointer commit and the listing of step 10, the generated files a conflict of step 3 may touch. It is **on** for a run when that section names the area of the entry and its conditions hold on `<base>` (step 4 looks once); `none`, or an area it does not name, skips every changelog step with one line in the report.

Run it in the root that section `Areas` names (the main checkout, or the workspace folder when the areas are repositories side by side), the one that holds the base branches; a worker folder of the loop (`worker.py`) cannot switch to them. Before the answer of step 5 you change nothing: only reads, `git -C <R> switch <base>` (the one change to the checkout; step 5 undoes it on `No`), the dry run of `worker.py merge`, the dry runs section `Changelog and version` names, and, after the owner's yes to the question of step 3 (a conflict found by the dry check), the `worker.py remarks` of that step.

## Steps

1. **Preconditions.** Every area repository is clean (`git -C <R> status --porcelain`; a repository that carries a shared submodule, section `Shared submodule`, with `--ignore-submodules=all`) and `todo ping` answers; otherwise stop with a message. Remember the starting branch of every area repository. Never `fetch` and never `push`.
2. **Choose the branch.** `git -C <R> switch <base>` for each area. `todo list --status review --area <area> --json` for each area of the rules file (only those: the store also holds other repositories' entries, `todo-loop/SKILL.md`, "Only the areas of the rules file"). Candidates: entries in `review` whose `branch` is in `git -C <R> branch --list 'todo/*' --no-merged <base>` of their area. Unmerged branches that are not candidates (their entry is `in progress ... stopped`, or open) are only named, with the `statusLine`.
   - A **stacked** branch (its `statusNote` ends with `stacked on todo/<parent>`: `todo-loop/SKILL.md`, "Stacked branches") is a candidate only when its parent is merged: the parent branch is gone or is in `git -C <R> branch --list 'todo/*' --merged <base>`. Otherwise it is named as `waits for todo/<parent>`; merging it first would bring the parent's commits in unreviewed by the owner and under the wrong entry.
   - An entry with an open blocker (a `blockedBy` that is not `closed`: a **paired** entry waits for the entry of the shared submodule it was built on, `todo-loop/SKILL.md`, "Paired", section `Shared submodule`) is a candidate only once the blocker is merged and closed; until then it is named as `waits for <id>`. Merge the submodule's entry first: its pointer commit is what the paired entry was built on.
   - A **feature** umbrella (`todo-loop/SKILL.md`, "Feature") is a candidate like any entry in review: its branch is `feature/<umbrella id>` (not `todo/*`: list it too, `git -C <R> branch --list 'feature/*' --no-merged <base>`), its children are closed already, each merged into the feature by its loop after `APPROVE`. The report shows the merged children (`git -C <R> log --first-parent --oneline <base>..feature/<id>`); the close closes the umbrella; the branch is deleted as any other.
   - With an argument (`<id>`, `todo/<branch-id>` or `feature/<id>`): take that branch. If it is not a candidate, stop and say why: no such branch, already merged into its base, the status is not `review`, or it is stacked on a parent that is not merged (name the parent: merge it first).
   - Without an argument: one candidate is taken as is; several candidates go to the owner in one AskUserQuestion (area, title, branch, the note of the status); none: say "nothing to merge" and stop.
3. **Dry check.** `worker.py merge <id> --dry-run --json` (from the root of section `Areas`; it reads only, runs `git merge-tree` and sees the loops). `appends` lists the shared docs (the shared files of section `Workers`: `CLAUDE.md`, `README.md` and the like) where both sides only added a block at the same place, as two entries appending to the same decisions file do: no stop, step 6 stitches them (the base's block first, then the branch's). `problems` stop (a dirty tree, a blocker not merged; a loop on the branch: the owner stops it first, name the worker). `conflicts` lists the files that stop the merge; the skill never resolves them and merges nothing. A conflict in exactly the generated files section `Changelog and version` names (the base moved and both sides carry a fragment) is no conflict: do what that section says for it first (for example install its merge driver, idempotent) and the dry check again, so the driver takes them. `moved` (the merge base of `<base>` and the branch is not the tip of `<base>`): "the base moved since the branch was cut" is a flag for the report and for step 6.
   **Conflicts: send the branch back to its worker.** Print, in Russian, the report of step 4 reduced to what the owner needs to decide: the entry, the branch, the conflicting files, where the branch can be tried (as in step 4), and that the base moved. Then **one** AskUserQuestion: `Send back to the worker (conflict round)` / `No`.
   - `No`: switch every area repository back to its starting branch (as the `No` of step 5) and stop.
   - Yes: `worker.py remarks <id> --file - --start`, the text on stdin from a heredoc (no file is written):

     ```
     **Conflict round**
     Merging <base> into todo/<branch-id> conflicts in: <files>. Merge <base> into the branch, resolve each conflict keeping what both sides meant, build, test, and commit the merge.
     ```

     For a stacked branch whose parent is merged since, add one line that `<base>` now holds the parent's commits. The command's answer is reported as it is (the plan name, the worker that holds the branch, whether a session started). When no worker holds the branch or the start is refused, the remarks plan is stored all the same and the report says that the owner starts the round (the panel of `worker.py`, or `/todo-loop <id>`: `todo-loop/SKILL.md`, "Remarks"). Then switch every area repository back to its starting branch and stop: no merge, no close, no delete, no changelog. The developer of the loop resolves the conflicts and the entry returns to `review` with the note `conflict round: <files> resolved`; the next `/todo-merge` takes it.
4. **Report.** In Russian, short, printed before the question:
   - the title of the entry, the area and the branch, and where it can be tried live: the worker folder that has it checked out (`git -C <R> worktree list`; the loops run in worker folders, `todo-loop/SKILL.md`, "Parallel workers"), else the root with `git -C <R> switch <branch>`;
   - if the `statusNote` has `slice K of M, after the merge set the status back to open`: a warning that the merge does not finish the entry (closing sets it back to `open` instead of closing it);
   - what was done and how it was checked: the `statusNote` of the entry;
   - `git -C <R> log --oneline <base>..<branch>` and `git -C <R> diff --stat <base>...<branch>`, plus two to four lines of your own on what changed, from the commit subjects and a look at the diff of the non-test files;
   - from the slice plan (`todo plan get todo-loop-<branch-id>.md --stdout`, if the store has it): "Review notes" (non-blocking findings; the `[followup]` lines are listed apart, with the rolling entry they will go to: `todo-loop/SKILL.md`, "Follow-ups", and step 7) and what the architect left for the owner to check by hand, with the link `todo plan url todo-loop-<branch-id>.md`;
   - decisions: the dated decision paragraphs of the entry's body (`todo show <id>`; "Decided by the owner", "Owner decision", "Taken over") in one line each (date, what it decides) and, from grepping the `*.md` of `<R>` and the titles of the open entries for their words, the places that still state a policy a later decision replaced (`file:line` or entry id); `none` when there are none. Reads only: a warning for the owner, never a stop;
   - screenshots (section `Shots` is not `none`): when `~/.claude/plans/todo-loop-<branch-id>-shots/` (a through entry: also `todo-loop-<branch-id>-s*-shots/`) exists, the folder as a `file://` link, the summary that section says its tool writes there and the changed pairs, and that the pictures are for the owner's eyes: do not open them. A folder that is not there on this machine (the plans folder is local) is one line, not an error;
   - the dry check (clean, or the docs it will stitch) and the "base moved" flag;
   - a busy worker, when the root has workers (`worker.py list` names some, as in step 11): the `warnings` of the dry check (a worker's loop runs in `<R>` on `<base>` or detached: the merge moves the base under it);
   - changelog on: the draft, the dry run and the forecast that section `Changelog and version` describes for this step, the draft written to the scratchpad and printed. The report calls a forecast a forecast: a number is given only in step 8, after the owner's yes; a refusal of the dry run is shown as it is and the draft is fixed first;
   - for an area that is a shared submodule of the root's repository (section `Shared submodule`): that the answer also makes a separate commit in that repository that moves the pointer, named as that section says.
5. **Ask.** One AskUserQuestion: `Merge + close + delete branch` (for a shared submodule's area: `+ move the pointer of <submodule>/`), `Merge only (no close, the branch stays)`, `No`. `No` ends the skill with nothing changed: switch every area repository back to its starting branch first, so the owner stays on the branch they were trying. When the full answer is chosen and the changelog is on, the further question section `Changelog and version` names (for example the bump: the proposed one first, the other as the alternative). `Merge only` writes no fragment.
6. **Merge.** `git -C <R> merge --no-ff -m "Merge branch '<branch>'"` on `<base>` with the Co-Authored-By line the conversation gives (the history uses this form). When the base moved and section `Changelog and version` names a merge step for that case (it derives generated files from the fragments), merge with `--no-commit`, run that step, then `git -C <R> commit --no-edit --cleanup=strip`. If the merge stops on conflicts and step 3 had `appends`: `worker.py stitch --repo <R>` (writes and stages only those docs; it refuses any other conflict and then writes nothing), then `git -C <R> commit -m "Merge branch '<branch>'"` with the same Co-Authored-By line; name the stitched files in the report. If the merge fails for any other reason, or `stitch` or the changelog's merge step refuses: `git -C <R> merge --abort`, switch back to the starting branches, report, stop. If the base had moved (step 3), also build and run the tests of section `Build and test (baseline and reviewer)` for `<R>` on the result; red tests: stop before step 7 (the close), name the failing tests, leave the merge commit (it is local; no reset, the owner decides).
7. **Close** (only for the full answer). Do step 3 of "Closing merged entries" in `todo-loop/SKILL.md` for the entry of this branch only: `todo close <id> --reopen` for a slice that does not finish the entry, else `todo close <id>`. Other entries that wait to be closed are named in the report and left alone. No commit: the store holds the status.
   Then file the review follow-ups, while the branch still exists: `worker.py followups <id> --branch <branch> --json` (the same code as the quick merge: the `[followup]` lines of "Review notes" of the slice plans go to the first open or deferred `<area>-review-followups`; `todo-loop/SKILL.md`, "Follow-ups"). `null` means none; otherwise name `entry` and `lines` in the report, and a finding repeated across branches as a rule to propose. Then record what the entry cost, also while the branch still exists: `worker.py stats record <branch-id> --entry <id>` from the root (tokens per role, reviews, blocking findings, changed lines; the record goes to the store, `todo stats report` reads it). A failure of either is a line in the report, never a stop (the merge stays). The report of step 13 gives the cost in one line (weighted tokens and dollars).
8. **Changelog and version** (only for the full answer, only when it is on). What section `Changelog and version` says for this step, with the draft of step 4 (with the owner's corrections, if the answer asked for any) and the answer of step 5: typically a number from the store's counter, the tool's files written and one commit on `<base>` with the Co-Authored-By line the conversation gives, nothing else staged. A failure stops this step only, with its message (the merge and the close stay; the report says what to rerun or fix by hand); a number is never made up locally. It comes after the close and before the branch is deleted, so its commit sits on the merged `<base>` and before the pointer commit of step 10.
9. **Delete the branch** (only for the full answer, after step 8). A branch checked out in a worker folder cannot be deleted: find it in `git -C <R> worktree list --porcelain` (for a shared submodule's area, the worktree is the submodule folder of the worker) and, when `git -C <worktree> status --porcelain` is empty, `git -C <worktree> switch --detach <base>`; when it is not empty, leave the branch and say which folder holds it. Then `git -C <R> branch -d <branch>`. On failure report it and leave the branch.
10. **Move the pointer** (only for the full answer and only for an entry of an area that section `Shared submodule` names; `none`: skip). The submodule now stands on the merged `<base>` (and on the commit of step 8, when there is one). In the repository that carries it, as that section says: before `git add <submodule>`, `<old>` = `git rev-parse HEAD:<submodule>`; one commit named as that section says (with the version of step 8 when there is one) with the Co-Authored-By line the conversation gives, on the branch that repository stands on (it is the owner's own; if that is not its base branch, say so in the report and still commit only the pointer); then the listing that section names since `<old>` (what the move breaks for the carrying repository), into the report of step 13. Nothing else is staged. The pointer is never moved before the merge, and never to a commit that is not in `<base>`.
11. **Start the idle workers** (only after the full answer, and only when the root has workers: `worker.py list` names some). `worker.py kick --merged <id>`: the workers that held the entry count their entries in a row from zero again, and every idle worker (finished, clean, its queue not paused) starts on the first takeable entry of its queue, detached (the store's docs, "Workers"). Name what it started and what it skipped; a failure is a note, never a stop.
12. **Perf sentinel** (optional; only after the full answer, and only when the repository has `.claude/rules/perf-project.md`). Its setting is the line "The sentinel (`todo-merge`)" of that file: `off` skips it, `always` runs it, `ask` (the default) asks one AskUserQuestion: `Run the fast perf set` / `Skip`. It runs the fast subset of the set of the root on the merge (after step 10, so the carrying repository is measured with the merged submodule), with the runner and the perf skill that file names (for example `python3 <engine>/tools/perf/perf.py --fast --json <scratchpad>/sentinel-<branch-id>.json`; the perf skill's step 1 first: a busy machine is reported, not measured). Exit 0: one line with the verdicts. Exit 3: repeat the regressed benchmarks once (`--only <names> --repetitions 10 --no-build`); if they still regress, file an entry through the `todo-add` steps with the id `perf-<branch-id>` (`-2` if taken), in the area of the merged entry, title `Perf regression after merging <branch>`, whose body names the merge commit, the baseline commit, the machine and, per regressed metric, the mean ± half-width, the baseline mean and the change, and ends with the investigate command that file names (for example "investigate: `/perf-investigate <benchmark>`"). Exit 1: report the error. The sentinel never reverts, never updates the baseline and never blocks: the merge stays.
13. **Report the result.** In Russian: the merge commit, the commit and the version of step 8 (or why there is none), the pointer commit and its listing, the workers the kick started, the new status of the entry (`closed`, or `open` for the next slice), the follow-ups filed (the rolling entry and the count, and a repeated finding proposed as a rule), the cost line, whether the branch was deleted, whether build and tests ran, where the checkout stands now (the base branch, since the work branch is gone) and the worker folder moved off it, if any. Name the other branches in `review` of the same area: their base has moved now, and their own dry check comes when they are merged (a branch whose dry check conflicts then is sent back to its worker as a conflict round, step 3, not only named). Name first the branches stacked on the one just merged (their note says `stacked on <this branch>`): they can be merged now, and the next `/todo-merge` takes them. End by saying that `git push` is the owner's.

## Forbidden

`git push`, `fetch`, `--force`, `reset --hard`, `branch -D`, `clean`, `stash`, `--no-verify`; resolving conflicts (the developer of `todo-loop` does, in a conflict round; `worker.py stitch` of step 6 and the merge driver of section `Changelog and version` aside); editing code or any file (the only file writes are the stitched docs of step 6, the files of step 8 that section `Changelog and version` names, committed on `<base>`, and the pointer of step 10; each stages only its own); a fragment before the merge or without the owner's answer of step 5; a write to the store other than the `close`, `followups` and `stats record` of step 7, the counter of step 8, the entry of the sentinel (step 12) and the `worker.py remarks` of step 3 (only after the owner's yes); merging a branch whose entry is not in status `review`; any change before the answer of step 5, except that `remarks` of step 3.
