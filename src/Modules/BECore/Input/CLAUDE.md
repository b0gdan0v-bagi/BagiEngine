# Input

`UIInput` (`CoreManager::GetUIInput()`) is the one door for mouse input. `SDLEventsProvider` (`SDL/CLAUDE.md`), `ViewportWidget` (the editor viewport, through `ViewportPointer::Forward`) and the control API's `ui_click`, `click_at` and `wheel_at` call `DispatchMouseButtonDown/Up`, `DispatchMouseMotion`, `DispatchMouseWheel`; none of them emits the `SDLEvents` mouse events itself. Positions are render output pixels.

## The walk

- Registered `IUIInputHandler`s are asked topmost first: the higher `order`, among equals the later registered. A handler is asked only when its `ContainsPoint(x, y)` holds (default `true`: a canvas decides for itself); the first `true` answer consumes the event. Only an event nobody consumed is emitted raw for game code, so a click on a button does not also hit the board under it. A press nobody consumed starts no capture.
- **The order key is the draw position of the handler's place:** `Scene::GetInputOrder(component)` = node draw rank times `Scene::kMaxComponentsPerNode` plus the component's index on the node. Canvases, RmlUi documents and scene targets all register with it and refresh it in `OnDraw()` (also while hidden) by `Unregister` + `Register` only when it changed, so they cover each other as they are drawn. A game component that listens to raw mouse events is the bottom of the order.
- **A scene target** is a `PointerTargetComponent` (`Scene/Components/`; `ClickableComponent` is its smallest subclass), a handler that registers in `OnAttached`, unregisters in its destructor, stamps `Scene::GetDrawPass()` in `OnDraw()` and keeps `HitClip::FromRenderer()` there. `ContainsPoint` is false unless its scene is active, it drew in the current pass (a hidden node or an inactive scene draws nothing, so takes nothing; a pass counter of 0, a test that never draws, counts as drawn) and the point is inside its clip and its shape. A game derives from it, never from `IUIInputHandler` directly, so the draw-order work cannot be forgotten: `OnAttached` and `OnDraw` are `final`, the derived class overrides `OnTargetAttached` / `OnTargetDraw`. The base is concrete (the reflection generator needs it for the factory): a bare one in a scene file takes the motion over its shape and no press or wheel.
  - **Shape** (`PointerShape`, scene units): the node's unrotated `TransformComponent` rect (default), an explicit rect, a circle or an ellipse inscribed in the rect (the node's when none is given), or `ContainsScenePoint` overridden. Edges are inside; a zero size or a NaN point is outside.
  - **Callbacks** carry a `PointerEvent` (scene and output position, `which`, `button`, `clicks`, `touch`): `OnPointerPress` returns `true` to take the press (it is then held and the target gets `OnPointerDrag` for every motion of that pointer and `OnPointerRelease` for its up, wherever the pointer is); `OnPointerMove` returns `true` (the default) to take a motion over the shape, so what is drawn below gets no hover; `OnPointerWheel` returns `true` to take the wheel (default no). `OnPointerEnter` comes with the first motion of a pointer on the shape, before `OnPointerMove`; a declined `OnPointerMove` (`false`) sends `OnPointerExit` at once, and what is below gets the motion; a taken one ends in `OnPointerExit` when the pointer goes to another handler or off the shape. A target that wants no hover at all answers `false` from `ContainsScenePoint`.
  - **The press list is `UIInput`'s:** a target cannot ask in `OnMouseButtonUp` whether a release is its own, because the dispatcher ended the capture before calling it, so `UIInput::HoldsPress(handler, which)` (a motion of its drag, not of a pointer that passes) and `IsDeliveringCapture(handler)` (true only inside the up of the held press) answer it. The release of another button or pointer, and a motion while a button of the game is held, are therefore the game's: **game code gets no raw down over a target's shape, and raw motion and up only of a press the game holds.**
  - **A callback may destroy the component** (a scene switch): the base touches no member after a callback that can be followed by another call, and a derived class copies what it needs before the call that may destroy it.
  - `ClickableComponent` (`_actionName`, `_shape` Rect or Ellipse, `_blocksInput`) emits the action on a press; `_blocksInput` takes the press and the motion over the shape even with no action, a shield for what is drawn below. Both fields are optional with the old behaviour as default, so an old file means the same and there is no migration step.
  - **`ClipRectComponent`** (no fields): the node's `TransformComponent` rect clips the node, its subtree, what it draws and what it hits: the targets read the renderer's clip as they draw, so a point outside the clip reaches the handler below. `SceneDrawSystem` applies it (a clip owner on each `SceneDrawItem`), because the draw order is by layer and not by subtree; nested clips intersect up to `SceneDrawSystem::kMaxClipDepth`.
- A handler answers `true` only for what it used. `OnPointerLeave(which)` is called once when the pointer it was over (the last motion it consumed) goes to another handler or to nothing.

## Capture and hover

`PointerTracker` keeps, per pointer (`which` of the event) and button, the handler that consumed a press, and per pointer the hover. The up of a held press goes to its holder first, wherever the pointer is and whatever `ContainsPoint` says, and is consumed whatever the holder answers (a release never leaks to the game or to a handler that did not see the press); the motion of a pointer that holds a press goes to the holder first, and the walk goes on without it when it answers `false`. A handler therefore needs no pressed state of its own for the release (a canvas keeps its own for its elements; the capture is the second line). `Unregister` forgets the handler's captures and hover at once, so a handler that re-registers to change its order loses a held press: do it only when the order moved.

## Pointers and touch

`which` is the pointer id (`PointerId.h`): a mouse instance id, `SDL_TOUCH_MOUSEID` and `SDL_PEN_MOUSEID` as SDL gives them, and a finger as `kFingerPointerBase` plus a place of `FingerPointers` (8 at once, the ninth gets no events; a place is reused once its finger is up). Capture and hover are per `which`, so two fingers hold two presses on two targets. The provider turns SDL finger events into the same motion, down and up as the mouse takes, under the same gates (pause, ImGui capture), a cancelled finger releases its press, and a lifted one ends its hover (`UIInput::EndHover`: SDL sends no leave). The mouse events SDL synthesizes from touch (`SDL_TOUCH_MOUSEID`) are dropped while SDL finger events are enabled, since they are the same touch again; a platform that only sends those keeps them as its single pointer. A trackpad, a pen's touch and a finger with no window are not fingers. The editor viewport has one pointer (ImGui has one) and passes the `which` of touch and pen. `PointerEvent::touch` is `IsTouchPointer(which)`.

## The point click

`click_at {x, y, space?, button?, which?}` (`InputControl.h`) and `ui_click` move, press and release through `UIInput` at a point, with the pointer `kControlPointerId`, as a person does; `space` is `output` pixels or `scene` units mapped by the active scene's last draw. "consumed" says whether a handler took the press; `UIInput::LastPressTaker()` names it (an identity to compare, it may be gone) and `ui_click` answers `takenBy` from it: `element` (its own canvas), `target` (a handler above it: `action` and `value` are empty, the element emitted nothing) or `none`.

## The wheel

`wheel_at {x, y, dx?, dy?, flipped?, which?}` (`InputControl.h`) sends one `SDL_MouseWheelEvent` through `DispatchMouseWheel` at an output pixel, `dx` / `dy` as SDL gives them (`dy` positive away from the user), `flipped` as `SDL_MOUSEWHEEL_FLIPPED`. It sends no motion first: the event carries its own position, and a handler that needs the pointer there (a document) moves it itself. "consumed" says whether a handler took it; otherwise game code gets the raw event. No `dx` and no `dy` is `invalid_params`.

## Rules

- Register and unregister inside a handler are safe: a registration joins after the dispatch in progress, an unregistered handler is not called again, not even by that dispatch, and is never called to leave. A handler that unregisters itself while answering `true` starts no capture.
- **A dispatch allocates nothing:** no container per event; `PointerTracker` holds `kMaxEntries` captures and hovers inline (a further one is refused and the dispatcher then simply holds nothing).
- Tests: `PointerTrackerTest`, `UIInputTest`, `UIInputPointerTest`, `SceneInputOrderTest`, `PointerTargetComponentTest`, `ClickableComponentTest`, `ClipRectComponentTest`, `FingerPointersTest`, `ClickAtControlTest`, `WheelAtControlTest`.
